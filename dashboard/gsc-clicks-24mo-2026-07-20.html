Reading the trend: more impressions, fewer clicks

Two things are moving in opposite directions here, and neither is subtle. Monthly impressions roughly doubled from their mid-2025 trough (~32–42M/month, Jun–Dec 2025) to 60–69M/month by Jan–May 2026 — and average ranking position improved at the same time, from roughly position 10–13 in mid-to-late 2025 to position 5–8 through 2026. Normally, better rankings plus more impressions should mean more clicks, not fewer. Instead, CTR compressed from ~2.0–2.9% in spring 2025 down to a floor of ~1.36–1.40% in Jan–Feb 2026 — and the YoY numbers above make the divergence explicit: March–May 2026 clicks are down 25–35% year-over-year in the same months impressions were up 4–41%.

Is it AI Overviews? It's a plausible contributor, and the shape of the data fits: getting more visibility and better positions while earning proportionally fewer clicks is the textbook signature of a SERP feature absorbing attention before the user reaches organic results. It also matters that GSC's impression metric doesn't require a result to be scrolled to — a listing pushed further down the page by an AI Overview block still counts as a full impression even if far fewer real users ever scroll to it or click through, having already gotten an answer from the AI box. That said, I checked whether GSC breaks out AI Overview appearances directly (via the searchAppearance dimension) for this property, and it doesn't — the only appearance types reported are minor ones (translated results, product snippets, forums, video), none of which explain a swing this size. So this remains a well-supported hypothesis, not a confirmed cause; other contributors could include broader/less-intent-matched query mix as impressions grew, other SERP features (People Also Ask, more sitelinks), or algorithm changes independent of AI Overviews.

Worth flagging: the trend isn't monotonic. June and July 2026 show recovery — June clicks are flat-to-up YoY (+1.5%) despite impressions still climbing (+17%), and July (partial-month, apples-to-apples) is actually up on both clicks (+19.6%) and impressions (+7.9%), meaning CTR itself improved year-over-year for the first time in this dataset. Whatever suppressed CTR through the Sep 2025–Feb 2026 stretch may be easing, not permanently structural — worth re-checking again once August closes out.
